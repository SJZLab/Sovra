## Why they are not equivalent mutants (Law 1: shown able to fail first)

`import_common.Slot.kind` is a plain `str` field (not a validated `Literal`/enum at the dataclass
level); only the strict parser `_slot()` constrains it to exactly `"key"`/`"header"`/`"index"`/
`"letters"`. The existing `test_non_key_slot_is_mapping_invalid` mixes a `"header"` slot with a
`"key"` slot, so under the M4 mutant the loop still raises `bad_slot` — just triggered by the
`"key"` slot instead of the `"header"` one — and the test only asserts `reason == "bad_slot"`, so
it can't tell the two branches apart. No test built a mapping where *every* slot is `"key"` (the
one legitimate case, which must NOT raise) or a slot whose `kind` differs from `"key"` only in
case/whitespace (which must still be refused, not normalized).

Added two tests to `tests/tasks/test_ftf_0503.py`:

- `test_all_key_slots_pass_options_without_raising` — a `prompt_completion` mapping where both
  `prompt` and `completion` are `{"key": ...}`. Kills M4: under the flipped `==`, the first `"key"`
  slot raises `bad_slot` even though the mapping is entirely valid.
- `test_slot_kind_match_is_exact_no_normalization` — builds a mapping via `dataclasses.replace`
  with a `Slot(" Key ", "q")` (bypassing `_slot()`'s own strict construction on purpose, since
  `Slot.kind` carries no runtime guarantee of its own). Kills M9: under `.strip().lower()`, `" Key
  "` would normalize to `"key"` and pass; the real check must refuse it (`bad_slot`).
